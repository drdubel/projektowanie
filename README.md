# projektowanie

3D-printable parts, modelled parametrically in [FreeCAD](https://www.freecad.org/) with Python scripts.

## Parts

| Part | Description |
| --- | --- |
| [`esp32c3-supermini-hmmd-case`](parts/esp32c3-supermini-hmmd-case) | Two-part case (base + lid) for an ESP32-C3 SuperMini Plus (red, V2) together with a Waveshare HMMD mmWave sensor. The GPIO header pins stick out through the lid; the sensor's antennas look down through a thin radome floor in the base. |
| [`esp32c3-supermini-case`](parts/esp32c3-supermini-case) | ESP32-only variant of the case, without the sensor bay. Same two-part design with the header pins on top. |
| [`esp32c3-ceiling-multisensor`](parts/esp32c3-ceiling-multisensor) | Round ceiling unit for a 70 mm hole in a two-layer ceiling (30 mm lower layer, gap, upper ceiling 40 mm up): ESP32-C3 SuperMini Plus, DHT22 module, MH-Z19B CO₂ sensor and the Waveshare HMMD, all within 38 mm of the ceiling surface. Three swing clamps open in the gap and grip the top of the lower layer. On the room side the HMMD looks out through a fixed radome face tilted 25°, beside the flush DHT22 grille. |

## Layout

Every part has its own folder under `parts/` that holds everything for that part:

```text
parts/<part-name>/
├── <script>.py        parametric FreeCAD script - the source of truth
├── <document>.FCStd   FreeCAD document, generated by the script
└── <export>.stl       export for slicing (where available)
```

Multi-part designs put one STL per printed part, already in print orientation, in an `stl/` subfolder.

All dimensions and tolerances are parameters at the top of each script: change one and re-run to rebuild the model. The script runs its own geometry checks and saves the `.FCStd` only if they all pass. The documents were saved with FreeCAD 1.1.

The STL in `esp32c3-supermini-hmmd-case` contains both bodies, the base and the lid, laid out as in the exploded view (lid above the base). Separate them in your slicer: the base is printed floor-down on a smooth bed (a textured sheet roughens the radome) and the lid top-down.

## Building a part

Open FreeCAD's Python console (View → Panels → Python console) and run the part's script, for example:

```python
exec(open("/Users/antek/projektowanie/parts/esp32c3-supermini-hmmd-case/esp32c3_supermini_case.py").read())
```

The script saves its `.FCStd` next to itself (`SAVE_PATH`). Both the `exec(...)` path above and `SAVE_PATH` are absolute; if you clone the repository somewhere else, update them.

## Ceiling multisensor

The ESP32-C3, DHT22, MH-Z19B and HMMD go into a 68 mm cylinder that fits a 70 mm hole in the ceiling. The hole goes through a 30 mm lower layer; above it is a gap, and the solid upper ceiling is 40 mm up. The unit reaches 37.9 mm above the ceiling surface, so it stays 2.1 mm clear of the upper ceiling. A 94 mm trim covers the edge of the hole. The HMMD and the DHT22 sit in the trim. The MH-Z19B sits on the deck of a sensor tower above them, with the ESP32 on a tray on top of it.

- **Clamps:** turn a trim screw clockwise and the nyloc nut swings its arm out into the gap, to a stop. The arm then pulls down onto the top of the lower layer and grips 8 mm past the edge of the hole. It works for a lower layer 28.5–30.5 mm thick. Turning anticlockwise swings the arm back in against a lid column and lifts it to the top lid, which is its top stop.
- **HMMD:** the HMMD sits in a fixed plug whose flat face is the radome (0.8 mm wall, 6.2 mm air gap, as in the other case). The face is tilted 25° and sticks out 10 mm below the trim. To aim it, turn the whole unit in the hole before you tighten the clamps. The plug goes in from the room side until it stops, and crush ribs hold it, so it can be swapped without opening the unit.
- **DHT22:** the grille sits flush in a window of the trim, in a closed bay beside the HMMD plug.
- **MH-Z19B:** it hangs chamber-down over vents in the tower deck, about 15 mm above the room. Room air reaches it through slots in the trim and the space under the deck.
- **ESP32 and power:** the ESP32 lies flat on a tray on top of the MH-Z19B frame. A 5 V cable runs in the gap and comes in through a slot at the top of the wall, soldered to the ESP32's 5V and GND pins. The USB-C is only for flashing before assembly; update the firmware over Wi-Fi after that.
- **Wiring:** there are no headers or DuPont plugs. Solder the leads straight into the ESP32 and HMMD pin holes and onto the DHT22 and MH-Z19B pins.

Hardware:

- Clamps: 3 × M3 × 40 countersunk (ISO 10642) and 3 × M3 nyloc nuts (DIN 985).
- Tower: 3 × M3 × 10 countersunk, from the deck into the housing.
- Lids: 3 × M3 × 10 countersunk for the top lid and 4 × M3 × 8 countersunk for the DHT22 bay lid and the MH-Z19B frame.

All the screws are countersunk so their heads sit flush. Apart from the clamp screws, they all go into 2.6 mm pilot holes.

Assembly:

1. Flash the ESP32 over USB-C. Once the unit is assembled, you can only update it over Wi-Fi.
2. Solder the leads to the DHT22 pins, lay it grille-down in its bay in the housing, screw the bay lid on, and lead the wires out through the notch.
3. Solder five leads to the HMMD's J2 pads. Put the HMMD into the plug with its antennas toward the flat face, resting on the three ledges. Push the keeper in and lead the wires out through the slot in its cap.
4. Feed the HMMD leads up through the opening in the trim, then push the plug in from the room side until it stops.
5. Thread the DHT22 and HMMD leads up through the slot in the deck and set the tower on the housing. Screw it down with the three M3 × 10 screws.
6. Push the three M3 × 40 screws in from the room side until their heads sit flush in the trim. Put a nyloc nut into each arm's pocket, then screw the arm a few turns onto its screw end from above, tucked in against its lid column.
7. Solder the leads to the MH-Z19B pins, put it chamber-down into its bay, then screw the frame on.
8. Lead the 5 V cable in through the slot at the top of the wall. Solder it and the other leads to the ESP32, then lay the ESP32 on its tray, components up, with the USB-C toward the deck slot.
9. Put the top lid on, with the cable in its notch, and screw it down.
10. Turn each clamp screw anticlockwise until it stops, so the arm is tucked in and up against the lid. Push the unit into the hole, turn it so the HMMD faces where you want, and tighten the three screws.

Print the housing trim face down and the tower deck down. Print the HMMD plug radome face down on a smooth bed. Print the other parts as exported, and the arm three times. None of the parts need supports.

Caveats:

- The design depends on the ceiling: a lower layer 28.5–30.5 mm thick, with the upper ceiling at least 38 mm up. For a different ceiling, change `CEIL_T_MIN`, `CEIL_T_MAX` and `ROOF_Z` and re-run the script.
- The bay fits only the pins version of the MH-Z19B. The JST version is 39.3 mm long and does not fit.
- The DHT22 module dimensions come from a retailer drawing. Measure yours; the window takes a 15.1 × 25.1 mm sensor body.
- The MH-Z19B samples the air under the deck, so it responds a little more slowly than a sensor in the trim would.
- If the HMMD plug sits loose (printers differ), add a drop of glue at its top edge.
- The ESP32 sits between the two ceiling layers. If Wi-Fi is weak there, use the U.FL socket with an external antenna led out through the cable slot.

## Adding a part

1. Create `parts/<part-name>/` (lowercase, hyphen-separated).
2. Put the script, the `.FCStd` it writes and any exports (`.stl`, `.step`, ...) in that folder, and point the script's `SAVE_PATH` into it.
3. Add a row to the table above.

FreeCAD backup files (`*.FCBak`) are git-ignored.
